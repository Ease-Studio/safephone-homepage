How to Spot a Fake Facebook Page: 6 Signs to Check

SafePhone · Updated September 27, 2026

Facebook is one of the platforms scammers abuse most to impersonate government agencies, businesses, banks, delivery companies and celebrities. Knowing how to spot a fake Facebook page helps you avoid losing money, leaking personal information or having your account hijacked. This article covers 6 signs to check, how to read a page's "Page Transparency" section, and what to do when you come across a scam page.

Who do fake Facebook pages impersonate?

Fake Facebook pages usually target names that make people either trust them or worry:

  • Government agencies: the police, the cybersecurity department, or "fund recovery support" for people who have already been scammed.
  • Banks and e-wallets: "customer support", "account unlocking", "low-interest loans".
  • Delivery companies, airlines, hotels and restaurants: take ticket, room or party bookings, then vanish after receiving the deposit.
  • Businesses and big brands: job offers, customer appreciation events, prize giveaways.
  • Celebrities: investment pitches, charity appeals, online courses.

Fake pages often run ads to reach exactly the right people — see how scam ads target you. Their posts tend to repeat the familiar phrases in our list of common scam tactics in 2026.

6 signs of a fake Facebook page

1. The page name mimics an agency, bank or trusted brand

A common trick is to give the page a name very close to a government agency, bank, company or reputable organization: adding or dropping a few characters, using different punctuation, or changing the spelling. Examples: "Cục An Ninh Mạng" ("Cybersecurity Department"), "Bộ Công An Hỗ Trợ Thu Hồi Vốn" ("Ministry of Public Security – Fund Recovery Support"), "Ngân Hàng ABC Hỗ Trợ Khách Hàng" ("ABC Bank Customer Support"). Don't rely on the name alone — check the other signs below too.

Also note: the police in Vietnam do not run Facebook pages that charge a fee to "help you get back money you lost to scammers". A page with a name like that is almost certainly a fund-recovery scam.

2. An unusual name-change history

Facebook lets you see a page's name history. Many scam pages are bought or repurposed after they already have a following. If a page used to sell clothes or cosmetics, or post entertainment content, and later renamed itself after a bank, government agency or financial institution, be especially careful. See how to check this in the Page Transparency section.

3. An inconsistent posting history

What a page posts usually reflects its purpose. If a page once posted entertainment, product listings or unrelated news, then suddenly switched to promoting investments, fund recovery or financial services, be cautious. A sudden change in topic, tone or target audience often means the page has changed hands or is being used for something else.

Quick tip: scroll down to the oldest posts and read the comments. Fake pages usually have few posts, a burst of new posts in a short period, comments turned off, or nothing but identical praise.

4. Heavily edited posts and implausible posting dates

Some scammers deliberately edit or update old posts to make the page look long-established and trustworthy. When reading a post, look for the "Edited" label (tap it to see the edit history) and check whether the current content fits the original posting date. Many posts that have been edited repeatedly — especially about promotions, investments or money transfers — are a red flag.

5. It doesn't match the official channel listed on the website

Businesses, banks and large organizations usually list their official Facebook page on their website (typically in the footer or on the Contact page). If a Facebook page claims to represent a brand but isn't listed on the official website, or the link on the website points to a different page from the one contacting you, verify further.

Make sure the website you're comparing against is also genuine — see how to check if a website is a scam. Scammers may even build a fake website that links to their fake page.

6. The payment account doesn't match the company name

This is one of the most important signs. When asking for a transfer, fake pages usually provide a bank account in a personal name rather than the name of the company or organization they claim to represent. Common excuses: "the accountant is on leave", "the company account is under maintenance", "it's just a temporary transfer".

Check the account holder's name shown in your banking app carefully before confirming the transaction, and only send money when the recipient's name matches the organization you're dealing with.

How to check "Page Transparency" on Facebook

This section lets you quickly check signs 1–3 above. Menu names may vary by app version:

  1. Open the page you want to check in the Facebook app or a browser.
  2. Go to the page's About (“Giới thiệu”) section.
  3. Find and open Page transparency (“Tính minh bạch của Trang”).
  4. Look at the creation date, the name-change history and the country/region of the people who manage the page (if Facebook shows it).
  5. If the page is running ads, you can view them in Meta's Ad Library.
How to read the results: a "bank" whose page was created only a few months ago, has changed its name several times, or is managed from an unrelated country is a warning sign. A blue verified badge adds credibility but is not an absolute guarantee — still compare it with the official website.

What to do when you find a fake page

  1. Stop the transaction: don't transfer money, don't send photos of your ID card (CCCD, Vietnam's citizen ID), and don't tap any links they send.
  2. Contact the real organization through its official hotline or website to verify.
  3. Report the page to Facebook: tap the three-dot icon on the page → Report Page (menu names may vary by version) and choose impersonation or scam as the reason.
  4. Warn your family and friends — especially if the page is running ads targeted at your area.
  5. If you've already sent money: call your bank's hotline immediately, save the evidence (screenshots of the page, messages, account numbers) and report it to the police.

Conclusion

No single sign proves for certain that a Facebook page is fake. But if a page shows several red flags at once — multiple name changes, an inconsistent posting history, vague contact details, requests to transfer money to a personal account — stop the transaction and verify through official sources before going any further.

FAQ

How can I tell if a Facebook page is real or fake?

Check the page name for small misspellings, review its name history and creation date under Page Transparency, compare it with the Facebook page listed on the official website, and make sure the name on the payment account matches the company name.

Where can I see a Facebook page's name history?

Open the page, go to About, then Page Transparency. Facebook shows the date the page was created and any name changes there. Menu names may vary by app version.

Does a blue verified badge mean a Facebook page is definitely real?

A blue badge adds credibility but is not an absolute guarantee. Still compare it with the Facebook page listed on the official website and check the payment account.

Is a Facebook page asking me to transfer money to a personal account a scam?

It is a very serious red flag, especially when the page claims to be a business, bank or government agency and gives excuses like the accountant being on leave or the company account being under maintenance. Stop the transaction and contact the organization through its official channels.

How do I report a fake page on Facebook?

Open the page, tap the three-dot icon, choose Report Page and select impersonation or scam as the reason. If you have lost money, save the evidence and report it to the police.
