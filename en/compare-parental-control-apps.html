Parental Control Apps for Android Compared: Qustodio, Norton Family, Bark, Google Family Link and SafePhone

SafePhone · Updated September 27, 2026

Choosing a parental control app isn't just about picking the one with the most features. These apps help parents manage how their kids use their phones — from screen time limits to content filtering and protection from online risks — but each takes a very different approach to how it works, how much data it collects, and what it costs. This article compares 5 popular Android apps against the same set of criteria so you can pick the right one for your family.

How we compared parental control apps

  • Content filtering (web and apps): how well it blocks inappropriate websites and apps.
  • Screen time limits: whether you can set usage hours or lock the device.
  • Activity monitoring: tracking messages, social media, and usage history.
  • Location: location tracking and safe zones (geofencing).
  • Security and scam protection: whether it protects against malicious links and phishing.
  • Personal data: what permissions the app needs and how much of your child's data it collects.
  • Language, cost, and availability in Vietnam.

The 5 popular parental control apps in detail

Prices below are approximate, converted to VND, and may vary by plan, number of devices, and time of purchase.

1. Qustodio

  • Pros: Strong web and app blocking; lets you monitor calls and messages; helps cut down social media time and manage overall screen time; tracks your child's location and routes, and sends alerts about unusual behavior.
  • Cons: Some features are limited on Android due to system policies. Qustodio is developed in Spain, so the blacklists it uses to block websites and calls may not reflect the scam landscape in Vietnam well. No Vietnamese language support, which is inconvenient for parents who aren't comfortable with English. The app needs extensive system permissions such as contacts, GPS location, and browsing history — worth weighing if you're concerned about privacy.
  • Personal data: photos and videos, web browsing activity, activity in other apps, GPS location, personal information, and device information.
  • Cost: about 2,500,000 VND per year (approximate).

Best for: English-speaking families who want a full monitoring suite and are comfortable sharing a lot of data.

2. Norton Family

  • Pros: Effective website blocking, detects signs of scams in content, lets parents review their child's search history, and sends alerts when something looks unusual.
  • Cons: Geared more toward monitoring your child's Internet activity than comprehensive security. No Vietnamese language support.
  • Personal data: photos and videos, web browsing activity, activity in other apps, GPS location, personal information, and device information.
  • Cost: about 400,000 VND per year (approximate).

Best for: English-speaking families who want to keep an eye on their child's schoolwork and online activity.

3. Bark

  • Pros: Uses AI to analyze messages and social media to detect dangerous content.
  • Cons: Doesn't block thoroughly; it mainly alerts you after something has already happened. No Vietnamese language support. Not available for download in many countries, including Vietnam — according to Bark's support page, the service is currently only available in the US, Australia, and South Africa.
  • Cost: about 2,500,000 VND per year (approximate).

Best for: older kids — it helps parents spot risks while still respecting privacy, since Bark doesn't demand deep access to the phone. However, families in Vietnam can't use it yet.

4. Google Family Link

  • Pros: Free, easy to use, and built into the Android ecosystem; good at managing apps and screen time.
  • Cons: Lacks advanced features such as deep web filtering or content analysis; no scam protection. Your child needs to use a parent-supervised Google account.
  • Personal data: photos and videos, audio or voice recordings, payment card information and purchase history, contacts and messages, web browsing activity, activity in other apps, GPS location, personal information, and device information.
  • Cost: free.

Best for: parents just getting started — a basic option for limiting screen time and approving apps.

5. SafePhone

  • Pros: Focused on security and scam protection: blocks scam websites, warns about malicious links from SMS, social media, and browsers; offers a trusted-sites-only mode; and blocks calls from unknown numbers. SafePhone sends alerts to a Slack channel you create yourself, which serves as a communication channel between family members — that's how it stays completely free. You get an alert when your child receives an unknown call or taps a suspicious link, so you can step in early. Vietnamese interface, developed in Vietnam.
  • Cons: No message monitoring, screen time management, or GPS tracking, since those aren't what the app is for. Currently Android only.
  • Personal data: kept to the minimum needed for scam protection. According to its privacy policy, SafePhone processes device information, the links that are opened (to check they're safe), and incoming phone numbers; the contacts-only calling mode additionally needs access to contacts.
  • Cost: free.

Best for: families who want to protect their kids from scams with a Vietnamese-language app that's light on privacy intrusion; pair it with Google Family Link if you need screen time limits.

Google Family Link + SafePhone: free and covers the essentials

For many families, this free pair covers most needs:

  1. Google Family Link: set daily screen time limits and bedtime, and require your child to ask permission before installing new apps.
  2. SafePhone: install it from Google Play, set it as the default browser so every link is checked before it opens, turn on ad blocking, and turn on blocking calls from numbers not in contacts.
  3. Alerts for parents: create a Slack channel for alerts, then turn on alerts when a family member taps a suspicious link and when your child receives a call from an unknown number.
Note: don't install two apps that both manage screen time or both act as the default browser, as they can conflict. Family Link and SafePhone handle different jobs, so they work fine side by side.

A note on privacy when using parental control apps

The more a parental control app monitors, the more sensitive data it collects about your child — photos, messages, location, browsing history — and sends to the developer's servers. Before installing one:

  • Read the app's Data safety section on Google Play to see what data is collected and shared.
  • Only turn on the features you really need, and switch off message monitoring if it isn't necessary.
  • With older kids, favor tools that block risks over reading their messages, and be open with them about what's being monitored.

Conclusion

Qustodio and Norton Family are strong at monitoring but cost money, lack Vietnamese, and collect a lot of data; Bark isn't available in Vietnam yet; Google Family Link is free but lacks deep web filtering and scam protection. If your priority is keeping your kids away from scam links, fake websites, and unknown callers, SafePhone combined with Google Family Link is a free, Vietnamese-language, privacy-friendly choice.

FAQ

Which parental control apps are free?

Google Family Link and SafePhone are both free. Family Link handles screen time limits and app approvals, while SafePhone blocks scam links, websites, and calls, and sends alerts to parents.

Can Bark be used in Vietnam?

Not yet. According to Bark's support page, the service is only available in the US, Australia, and South Africa.

Can Google Family Link block scam websites?

Family Link has content filters for Chrome, but it isn't designed to detect scam or phishing sites. You can add SafePhone to check every link before it opens.

Should I use an app that reads my child's messages?

It depends on their age. With older children, reading their messages can damage trust. Tools that block scam links and alert you when there's a risk usually strike a better balance between safety and your child's privacy.
