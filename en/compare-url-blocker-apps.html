Scam Website Blocker Apps for Android Compared: BlockSite, Chrome Safe Browsing and SafePhone

Why do you need a scam website blocker?

Accidentally visiting malicious or fake websites is a major cause of fraud and data loss, especially for children and older adults — people with less experience of technology and the Internet. Scam links can arrive via SMS, Zalo, Facebook comments, or scam ads that target you, and many of these pages look exactly like a real bank's site.

Knowing how to check if a website is a scam is an essential skill, but not everyone has time to check before tapping. A website blocker detects and stops access to dangerous pages from the outset, adding an extra layer of protection for when you slip up.

How we compared website blocker apps

We evaluated the apps on four criteria:

  • Automatic blocking: does it detect and block dangerous websites on its own, or do you have to add each site yourself?
  • Database: is the list of scam websites updated regularly?
  • Scope of protection: does it only block websites, or does it also protect links in SMS, apps, and social media — and does it guard against other threats such as scam calls?
  • Ease of use: is the interface simple enough for everyday users and older adults?

BlockSite: manual blocking, good for content control

BlockSite blocks websites based on a list you create yourself. It's commonly used to limit distracting sites or unwanted content.

  • Pros: easy to use, lets you manually block specific websites, good for personal content control.
  • Cons: no automatic scam website detection; relies entirely on the list you build.

In other words, BlockSite can only block sites you already know about. With new scam sites popping up every day — often switching domain names constantly — manual blocking can't keep up.

Chrome Safe Browsing: a built-in warning layer on Android

The Chrome browser includes Google's Safe Browsing feature. Chrome uses a list of dangerous websites to warn you before you visit them.

  • Pros: built into Android, nothing extra to install; warns you when you visit a scam or malware site.
  • Cons: it only warns — users can still click past the warning and open the page — and it only protects links opened in Chrome, so it doesn't block system-wide.
Tip: if you use Chrome, make sure Safe Browsing is on: open Chrome → three-dot menu → Settings → Privacy and security → Safe Browsing (menu names may vary by version). Avoid choosing “No protection.”

Also, Safe Browsing's list is global, so scam sites targeting Vietnamese users specifically may not be added in time. Scammers also use many camouflage techniques used by scam websites to slip past automated scanners.

SafePhone: all-round security that works like a firewall

SafePhone acts as a “firewall” on your phone, blocking access to fake websites and warning you about malicious links. SafePhone focuses on collecting the addresses of websites suspected of being scams, malicious, or harmful. When you tap a link to one of those addresses, SafePhone warns you and blocks access.

  • Pros: beyond blocking malicious URLs, SafePhone also blocks scam calls and warns about dangerous links opened from many sources, such as SMS and social media.
  • Cons: it needs to be set as the default browser to work well; links opened in the built-in browsers inside some apps (such as Facebook, Messenger, and Zalo) don't pass through SafePhone.

Why does it need to be the default browser? On Android, that's how an app can receive and check a link before the web page opens — see the explanation in the URL screening permission on Android and iOS. Step-by-step setup is in the guide how to block scam websites.

Besides the blacklist, SafePhone offers other protection modes:

  • Block harmful sites and allow only trusted websites — blocks every unreviewed page by default, ideal for children.
  • Block ads on Android — blocks Facebook and Google Ads links that may lead to fake pages.
  • Analyzes the content of websites not yet on the list for signs of brand impersonation — details in how SafePhone works.

Which website blocker app should you choose?

  • Just need to block a few specific websites (social media, distracting entertainment sites): an app like BlockSite is enough.
  • Want a basic warning layer against scam websites: turn on Safe Browsing in Chrome.
  • Want more complete protection (URLs + calls + links in messages): SafePhone is the right fit.
  • Protecting young children or elderly parents: use SafePhone in trusted-sites-only mode, together with alerts when a family member taps a suspicious link.

These options aren't mutually exclusive: you can keep Chrome's Safe Browsing on while using SafePhone as your default browser. Whichever app you use, keep up safe browsing habits — never enter passwords or OTP codes on a page opened from an unfamiliar link.

Conclusion

BlockSite suits manually blocking a few specific websites, Chrome Safe Browsing is a basic built-in warning layer, and SafePhone aims for broader protection: blocking scam websites with a frequently updated blacklist, plus scam call blocking in the same app. Choose the tool that fits your needs, and protect the family members most vulnerable to scams first.

FAQ

What is the best website blocker app for Android?

It depends on your needs. BlockSite is good for manually blocking a few specific sites, Chrome Safe Browsing warns you about dangerous pages when you use Chrome, and SafePhone blocks scam websites using a blacklist and also blocks scam calls.

Does Chrome block scam websites automatically?

Chrome has a Safe Browsing feature that warns you when you open a scam or malware page on Google's list. However, it's only a warning that users can ignore, and it only applies to links opened in Chrome.

Can BlockSite block scam websites?

BlockSite blocks the sites you add to your list yourself. It has no automatic scam website detection, so it struggles to keep up with new scam sites as they appear.

Can I use SafePhone and Chrome at the same time?

Yes. Set SafePhone as your default browser so links are checked before they open, and you can still keep Chrome's Safe Browsing turned on as an extra layer of protection.
