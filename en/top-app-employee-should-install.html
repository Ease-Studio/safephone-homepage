The Best Apps for Office Workers to Boost Productivity and Security

SafePhone · Updated September 27, 2026

In the digital age, apps for office workers play a key role in boosting productivity, managing time, and keeping information secure. Your phone is both a work tool and the place where you read email and open documents — which also makes it a target for scammers. This article recommends 5 categories of must-have apps and explains how to use your phone for work safely.

1. Task management and note-taking apps

Task management apps make it easier to track progress, hit deadlines, and coordinate with your team.

  • To-do lists and project management: Todoist and Microsoft To Do work well for personal tasks; Trello and Asana are better for managing team tasks and projects.
  • Quick notes: Notion, Evernote, and Google Keep help you capture ideas and meeting notes.

Tip: pick one place as the “single source of truth” for your work. Juggling too many to-do apps makes it easy for tasks to slip through the cracks.

2. Communication and collaboration apps

Team collaboration apps speed up communication and cut down on email.

  • Work email: Gmail and Outlook let you handle work anytime, anywhere.
  • Team chat: Slack, Microsoft Teams, and WhatsApp make it quick to talk with coworkers.
  • Video meetings: Zoom and Google Meet make online meetings easy.

Slack can also serve as a channel for security alerts — see how to create a Slack channel for alerts from SafePhone.

3. Storage and document editing apps

Cloud storage lets you access your files from any device.

  • File storage: Google Drive, Dropbox, and OneDrive sync your files between computer and phone.
  • Document editing: Microsoft Office, Google Docs, WPS Office, and PDF readers let you work directly on your phone.

Note: document-sharing links (“You've been invited to view a document…”) are one of the most common phishing lures. Fake Google and Microsoft sign-in pages look almost identical to the real ones — check the domain before entering your password.

5. Focus apps

Focus apps help you cut distractions and manage your time better.

  • Distraction blockers: Forest and Focus To-Do help you stay off your phone while working.
  • Time tracking: Toggl Track and Clockify help you log and manage your working hours systematically.

Signs of a phishing email impersonating a partner or coworker

Work email is full of links and attachments, which makes it a prime target for phishing that impersonates partners, managers, or the IT department. Be on guard when an email:

  • Comes from an address that looks almost like your company's or a partner's domain (one character off, a different domain extension), or from a personal Gmail account claiming to be a director.
  • Makes an urgent request: a wire transfer, a change to a supplier's bank account details, or buying gift cards.
  • Says your “mailbox is almost full” or your “password has expired” and includes a sign-in link.
  • Has a compressed attachment, a file with an unusual extension, or an Office document asking you to “Enable Content” (turn on macros).
  • Contains a shortened link, or a link whose domain doesn't match the email's content.

When in doubt, verify through another channel (call a number you already know) and don't reply to the email directly. Read more in How to check if a website is a scam and Advanced high-tech scams. If you're a manager, also check out common security gaps in businesses.

Work phone security checklist

  1. Turn on two-factor authentication (2FA) for work email, Slack/Teams, and cloud storage.
  2. Only install apps from Google Play or your company's app store.
  3. Keep your operating system and apps up to date.
  4. Set a link-checking app as your default browser to block scam pages before they open.
  5. Don't reuse work passwords for personal accounts; consider a password manager.
  6. Lock your screen with a PIN or biometrics, and tell IT right away if you lose your phone.
  7. Avoid habits that expose your personal data, such as sharing documents publicly or posting photos that show internal information.

FAQ

Which apps should office workers install on their phone?

A basic kit includes an email app (Gmail or Outlook), team chat (Slack, Teams, or WhatsApp), task management (Trello, Todoist), cloud storage (Google Drive, OneDrive), and a security app that blocks scam links, such as SafePhone.

How can I spot a phishing email impersonating a business partner?

Check the sender's address carefully, and be wary of urgent payment requests or changes to bank account details, unfamiliar login links, and attachments that ask you to enable macros. When in doubt, verify by phone using a number you already know.

Is opening email links on a phone riskier than on a computer?

The small screen makes it hard to see the full web address, and people tend to be less careful when reading email on their phone. That's why it's worth using an app that checks links before opening them.

Should I use my personal messaging app for work?

Many companies still use consumer messaging apps such as WhatsApp or Telegram, but keep work accounts or groups clearly separate, turn on two-factor authentication, and follow your company's data security policy on sharing internal documents.
