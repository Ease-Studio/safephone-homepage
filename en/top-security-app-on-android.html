Best Security Apps for Android: Antivirus, Scam Website Blocking, App Lock, and VPN

Android phones are increasingly targeted by viruses, malware, and online scams. This article recommends standout apps in 5 security categories and explains how to combine them to fit your needs.

What does an Android phone need protection from?

Before choosing a phone security app, identify the risks you need to address. Each category of apps below tackles a different type of risk:

  • Malware: fake apps and unknown APK files that steal data or take control of your phone — see types of malware.
  • Scam and phishing websites: links in text messages, emails, and ads that lead to fake bank pages designed to steal passwords and OTP codes.
  • Unauthorized access: someone else picks up your phone and opens your banking app, email, or private photos.
  • Unsafe networks: public WiFi in coffee shops and airports can be eavesdropped on.
  • Tracking and data collection: apps and websites sending your data somewhere without your knowledge — see also what cookies are.

1. Android antivirus apps: detect and remove malware

Android antivirus apps scan apps and files to detect and remove malware.

  • Kaspersky Mobile Security: detects viruses, provides real-time protection, and blocks dangerous threats.
  • Avast Mobile Security: scans for viruses, locks apps, and checks WiFi network safety.
  • Bitdefender Mobile Security: strong security with a light performance footprint, suitable for multiple devices.

These apps come in free and paid versions; specific features vary by plan, so check the Google Play description before installing. Note: antivirus apps are strongest against known malware; they can't replace the habit of spotting malicious apps before you install them.

2. Apps that block scam websites while you browse

Web browsing protection keeps you from landing on fake or malicious pages — the most common risk for users in Vietnam today.

  • SafePhone: warns about scam websites, blocks malicious links, and keeps you safe when opening links from emails, text messages, or social media. When set as your default browser, SafePhone checks every link against a list of scam sites before opening it — see how to block scam websites. SafePhone can also block ads on Android that lead to scam pages, block scam calls, and send alerts to family members.
  • Google Safe Browsing: built into Chrome, it warns you about dangerous websites on Google's list. It's already there, no extra install needed.

These two tools complement each other: Safe Browsing relies on a global list, while scam sites targeting Vietnamese users pop up every day and may not have made it onto that list yet. See a detailed review in our comparison of scam website blocker apps, and learn how SafePhone works.

3. App lock and privacy apps

App lock apps protect your personal data from unauthorized access when someone else is holding your phone.

  • AppLock: locks apps with a password, fingerprint, or face recognition.
  • Norton App Lock: protects important apps such as banking and email.

Google Play has many apps called “AppLock” from different developers — choose one with lots of downloads, good reviews, and no permission requests beyond what app locking needs. Many Samsung, Xiaomi, and OPPO phones also have a built-in app lock or secure folder feature in Settings (the menu name may vary by version).

4. VPN and network security apps

A VPN encrypts your internet connection, protecting your data when you use public WiFi.

  • NordVPN: strong security, fast speeds, and many servers worldwide.
  • ExpressVPN: easy to use, good for beginners.

Both are paid services. Avoid “free VPN” apps of unknown origin: a VPN provider can see your traffic, and some fake VPN apps are actually malware. A VPN protects the connection, but it does not block scam websites if you tap on them yourself.

5. Network monitoring and tracker-blocking apps

These apps show you which apps are sending data out and reduce tracking while you browse.

  • GlassWire: monitors each app's network traffic and detects unusual activity, such as an unfamiliar app suddenly using a lot of data.
  • DuckDuckGo Privacy Browser: blocks trackers and protects your privacy while browsing.

To manage each app's permissions, you don't need to install anything extra: Android has a built-in Permission manager (see the built-in features section below).

Which Android security apps should you install?

Installing too many security apps can slow your phone down and drain the battery. Suggestions by need:

  • Everyday users: Google Play Protect (built in) + a scam website blocker like SafePhone. This pair covers the two most common risks: malicious apps and scam links.
  • Frequent public WiFi users: add a reputable VPN.
  • People who lend or share their phone: add an app lock for banking, email, and photos.
  • People who often install apps from outside Google Play: add an antivirus app — and ideally, drop that habit.
  • Elderly parents and children: prioritize blocking unknown calls and scam websites — see apps to protect elderly parents and apps to protect children on phones.

Office workers and crypto investors face additional risks of their own; see security apps for office workers and security apps for crypto traders.

Built-in Android security features you should turn on

Before installing extra apps, make sure Android's free layers of protection are turned on (menu names may vary by manufacturer and Android version):

  1. Google Play Protect: open Google Play → your profile picture → Play Protect, and turn on app scanning.
  2. System updates: Settings → System or Software update (“Cài đặt” → “Hệ thống” / “Cập nhật phần mềm”); install the latest security patch.
  3. Permission manager: Settings → Security & privacy → Permission manager (“Bảo mật và quyền riêng tư” → “Trình quản lý quyền”); revoke SMS, contacts, location, and accessibility permissions from apps that don't need them.
  4. Block installs from unknown sources: don't grant the “Install unknown apps” permission (“Cài đặt ứng dụng không xác định”) to your browser, Zalo, or Messenger.
  5. Lock your screen with a PIN or biometrics, and turn on Find My Device so you can lock or wipe your phone remotely if you lose it.
Note: the Accessibility permission lets an app read your screen and act on your behalf. Many banking malware strains trick users into granting it — only grant it to apps you truly trust.

Conclusion

Using security apps for Android is an important step in protecting your device and personal data. No single app does everything: turn on Android's built-in protections first, then add antivirus, a VPN, or malicious link blocking depending on your needs. FAQ

Does an Android phone need an antivirus app?

If you only install apps from Google Play and have Play Protect turned on, an antivirus app isn't essential. If you often install apps from outside Google Play or share your phone, a reputable antivirus app adds an extra layer of protection.

Can antivirus apps block scam websites?

Some antivirus apps include web protection, but their focus is malware. To block scam links effectively, add a dedicated scam website blocker like SafePhone or turn on Google Safe Browsing in Chrome.

Should I use a free VPN?

Be careful. A VPN provider can see your traffic, and some free VPN apps of unknown origin collect data or contain malware. Choose a reputable provider.

Is it better to install several security apps at once?

Not necessarily. Multiple apps with the same function can conflict, slow down your phone, and drain the battery. Pick one app per category based on the risks you actually need to address.
