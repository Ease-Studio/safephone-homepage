Common Scam Tactics in 2026: Red-Flag Phrases That Give a Scam Away

SafePhone · Updated September 27, 2026

Scammers constantly change phone numbers and domains, but the common scam tactics in 2026 still revolve around a familiar set of phrases: “việc nhẹ lương cao” (easy job, high pay), “làm nhiệm vụ nhận hoa hồng” (do tasks, earn commission), “lợi nhuận cam kết” (guaranteed returns), “cập nhật sinh trắc học” (update your biometrics), “công an điều tra” (the police are investigating)… This article collects the red-flag phrases that give a scam away in Vietnam — keeping the original Vietnamese so you'll recognize what you actually hear — explains the scenario behind each one, and shows you how to respond safely.

The safest response: hang up, leave the conversation, verify independently through official channels, and tell your family or the nearest police station.

Why do scammers keep using the same phrases?

In many scams, criminals rely not just on technology but on human psychology. They use phrases that play on fear (being investigated, having your power cut off, having your account locked), greed (high returns, easy jobs with high pay), curiosity (an unexpected order, a gift) or the wish to recover what you've lost. These phrases repeat across so many cases that experienced people spot the warning sign the moment they hear them.

Keep in mind: these phrases are not always a bad sign. “Cập nhật sinh trắc học” (biometric update), “sở hữu kỳ nghỉ” (timeshare) and “thu hồi tài sản” (asset recovery) can all appear in legitimate business. It's precisely because they sound familiar that scammers exploit them to build trust and hide their real intentions.

The 2024–2026 period has seen an explosion of new scam types and variations on old scripts, turning up constantly in calls, text messages, emails and social media posts — including scam ads targeted right at you and fake Facebook pages impersonating agencies and businesses. Understanding what each phrase really means helps you spot a scam before you become a victim.

“Fund recovery” scams (“thu hồi vốn treo”, “lấy lại tiền bị lừa”)

This scam has grown rapidly in 2024–2026 because it targets people who have already been scammed. After someone loses money to a fake investment, crypto, forex platform or online task scam, their details may be sold or traded among criminal groups.

The scammers reach out on their own, claiming to be lawyers, financial experts, asset recovery companies or even the authorities, and promise they can get the lost money back with a very high success rate.

Common phrases

  • Thu hồi vốn treo (recover frozen funds)
  • Lấy lại tiền bị lừa (get back money you lost to scammers)
  • Khôi phục tài sản (asset recovery)
  • Hoàn tiền 100% (100% refund)
  • Hỗ trợ pháp lý (legal support)
  • Phí hồ sơ (application fee)
  • Phí xác minh (verification fee)
  • Chuyên gia truy vết dòng tiền (money-tracing expert)

The script usually ends with the victim paying fee after fee and losing money a second time. If you've been scammed, report it directly to the police — never pay anyone who promises online to “get your money back”.

“Do tasks, earn commission” scams (“làm nhiệm vụ nhận hoa hồng”)

This has been one of the most common scam models on Telegram, Zalo, Facebook and TikTok in recent years. At first, victims are invited to do simple things like liking videos, reviewing products or following social media accounts.

To build trust, the scammers pay a small amount for the first few tasks. Then the victim is asked to deposit money to join “special tasks” with higher commissions.

Common phrases

  • Làm nhiệm vụ (do tasks)
  • Nhận hoa hồng (earn commission)
  • Việc nhẹ lương cao (easy job, high pay)
  • Tăng tương tác (boost engagement)
  • Like video kiếm tiền (make money liking videos)
  • Nạp tiền mở khoá (deposit to unlock)
  • Nhiệm vụ VIP (VIP tasks)
  • Hoàn thành đơn hàng (complete the order)
  • Hệ thống bị lỗi, cần nạp thêm (system error, you need to deposit more)

As the deposits grow, the system keeps reporting errors or demanding new tasks before any withdrawal is allowed, trapping the victim in a spiral of deposits. This script is often carefully played out over several days to build trust — see more advanced high-tech scams.

“Easy job, high pay” (“việc nhẹ lương cao”) and fake recruiters

Easy job, high pay (“việc nhẹ lương cao”)

“Việc nhẹ lương cao” (easy job, high pay) is a phrase that shows up constantly in online job scams. Scammers post listings for freelancers, remote workers or part-time jobs with attractive pay but very few skill requirements and almost no hiring criteria.

These listings usually target students, homemakers, job seekers and people looking for side income, promising anywhere from a few hundred thousand to a few million dong a day just for liking videos, reviewing products, data entry, closing sales or helping place orders.

  • Việc nhẹ lương cao (easy job, high pay)
  • Làm việc tại nhà (work from home)
  • Thu nhập 500.000đ – 2.000.000đ/ngày (earn 500,000–2,000,000 VND a day, roughly US$20–80)
  • Không cần kinh nghiệm (no experience needed)
  • Nhận việc ngay (start right away)
  • Cộng tác viên online (online freelancer)
  • Lương chuyển khoản trong ngày (paid by transfer the same day)
  • Chỉ cần điện thoại thông minh (all you need is a smartphone)
  • Tuyển dụng số lượng lớn (hiring in large numbers)
  • Đăng ký miễn phí (free sign-up)

Once they've earned your trust, the scammers ask for an application fee, a training fee, an account activation fee, or for you to join “advanced tasks” that require paying up front. Victims may receive a small payout at first, but as the deposits grow, withdrawing becomes difficult or impossible.

A simple rule: if a job promises pay that's far too good for the effort involved, it's most likely not a job opportunity but a trap. Don't transfer money, pay a deposit or share financial information before you've verified the recruiter's identity and legitimacy.

Recruiters impersonating big companies

More and more scammers pose as recruiters for big companies, using real logos, office photos, websites or employee names to look professional. Victims get messages via Facebook, Zalo, Telegram, LinkedIn or email saying their application has been selected, or offering an attractive job without the usual hiring process.

  • Nhà tuyển dụng, bộ phận nhân sự, HR tuyển dụng (recruiter, HR department, HR hiring)
  • Được chọn vào vòng tiếp theo (selected for the next round)
  • Phỏng vấn online (online interview)
  • Nhận việc ngay, thu nhập hấp dẫn (start right away, attractive pay)
  • Đóng phí đào tạo (pay a training fee)
  • Đặt cọc thiết bị làm việc (deposit for work equipment)
  • Kích hoạt tài khoản nhân viên (activate your employee account)
  • Chuyển khoản xác nhận hồ sơ (transfer to confirm your application)

Some scammers even create email addresses or websites with names almost identical to the real company — just a few characters off in the domain, which inexperienced users find very hard to spot. See how to check if a website is a scam to avoid this trap.

The biggest red flag is when a “recruiter” asks candidates to pay money in any form: application fees, uniform fees, training fees, insurance fees, equipment fees or a deposit to hold your place. Reputable companies generally don't charge candidates to hire them. When you get a job offer, check the listing on the company's official website, verify the contact person's email address and call the company's publicly listed phone number yourself.

“Guaranteed returns” (“lợi nhuận cam kết”) and fake investments

In financial investing, nobody can guarantee returns. So when you hear “lợi nhuận cam kết” (guaranteed returns), be on guard. Scammers promote investment schemes with unusually high returns and claim there's virtually no risk.

  • Lợi nhuận cam kết, bảo đảm lợi nhuận (guaranteed returns, profit guaranteed)
  • Lãi cố định, lãi suất cao (fixed interest, high interest rates)
  • Không rủi ro, đầu tư an toàn (no risk, safe investment)
  • Thu nhập thụ động (passive income)
  • Robot giao dịch (trading bot)
  • Hoàn vốn nhanh (fast payback)

These are hallmarks of Ponzi schemes, financial pyramid schemes and fake investment platforms. If you trade crypto, see also security apps for crypto traders.

Timeshares (“sở hữu kỳ nghỉ”) and free vacation vouchers

“Sở hữu kỳ nghỉ” (timeshare) is a legal business model in many countries. In Vietnam, however, the phrase often comes up in controversial cases involving exaggerated advertising or non-transparent sales pitches.

People are typically invited to a travel presentation, told they've won a prize, or given a free vacation voucher. Once there, they're pressured into signing a high-value contract with promises of long-term vacation benefits.

  • Sở hữu kỳ nghỉ, timeshare (vacation ownership, timeshare)
  • Voucher nghỉ dưỡng, du lịch miễn phí (resort voucher, free trip)
  • Nghỉ dưỡng 5 sao (5-star resort stay)
  • Ưu đãi duy nhất hôm nay (today-only offer)
  • Đặt cọc giữ quyền lợi (deposit to secure your benefits)
  • Nâng hạng thành viên (membership upgrade)

Any program that pressures you to sign a contract or pay on the same day deserves careful scrutiny. Take the contract home, read it carefully and talk to your family before signing.

“Biometric update” scams (“cập nhật sinh trắc học”)

Since 2024, biometric authentication rules for banking have been rolled out widely in Vietnam. Scammers quickly took advantage of the change: victims get a text or call asking them to update their biometrics through a strange link or app.

  • Cập nhật sinh trắc học, xác thực khuôn mặt (update your biometrics, face verification)
  • Kích hoạt tài khoản, xác minh thông tin (activate your account, verify your information)
  • Cập nhật CCCD (update your citizen ID card)
  • Tài khoản sẽ bị khoá (your account will be locked)
  • Đồng bộ dữ liệu dân cư (sync your residency data)
  • Đăng nhập xác thực (log in to verify)

The end goal is usually to steal your banking login or trick you into installing malware that takes control of your phone — see how to detect malicious apps. Biometric updates should only ever be done inside the official banking app downloaded from the app store, or at a bank branch.

Impersonating the police or the procuracy

This is one of the longest-running scripts, yet it keeps claiming new victims. Scammers exploit fear of law enforcement to create enormous psychological pressure: victims are told they're linked to a criminal case, that their bank account was used for money laundering, or that they're under investigation.

  • Công an điều tra, viện kiểm sát, toà án (police investigation, the procuracy, the court)
  • Lệnh bắt giữ (arrest warrant)
  • Rửa tiền, đường dây ma tuý (money laundering, drug ring)
  • Chuyên án bí mật, điều tra đặc biệt (secret case, special investigation)
  • Tài khoản nghi vấn (suspicious account)
  • Không được tiết lộ (you must not tell anyone)

An especially suspicious sign is being told to keep everything absolutely secret and not talk to your family or bank. In reality, the authorities never ask citizens to transfer money to a personal account for an investigation, and they don't work through video calls or chat apps. This script is sometimes combined with AI-cloned voices and faces — see advanced high-tech scams.

Fake electricity company payment demands

Impersonating electricity company staff is a scam that has kept resurfacing for years. Scammers call or text to say your electricity bill is overdue, your account is unpaid, or your power will be cut off very soon unless you act now. It works because every household uses electricity and not everyone remembers their exact due date.

  • Tiền điện quá hạn, hoá đơn điện chưa thanh toán (overdue electricity bill, unpaid bill)
  • Ngành điện lực, điện lực khu vực (the electricity sector, your regional power company)
  • Ngắt điện khẩn cấp, cắt điện trong hôm nay (emergency disconnection, power cut today)
  • Xác nhận thanh toán, đường link thanh toán (payment confirmation, payment link)
  • Cài đặt ứng dụng điện lực (install the electricity company app)
  • Chuyển khoản ngay để tránh bị cắt điện (transfer now to avoid disconnection)

Some scammers ask you to install an app from a strange link, claiming it's needed to process payment or verify your account. In reality it may be malware designed to take over your phone and steal your banking password or OTPs — learn more about types of malware.

Warning signs: constant time pressure such as “your power will be cut in 5 minutes” or “you must pay during this call”. Legitimate power companies have clear notification procedures and never ask customers to install apps via links sent from personal numbers. End the call and check for yourself using the power company's official app, your paper bill or its publicly listed customer service hotline.

Fake Shopee orders and delivery drivers

The e-commerce boom has brought a wave of fake orders from Shopee, Lazada, TikTok Shop and delivery companies. Scammers call or text to say you have an order on its way, an order that's been paid for, or a gift package about to arrive. Since many people can't remember exactly what they've ordered, quite a few go along with it.

  • Đơn hàng Shopee, đơn hàng đang giao, kiện hàng của bạn (your Shopee order, order out for delivery, your package)
  • Shipper giao hàng, xác nhận nhận hàng (delivery driver, confirm receipt)
  • Thanh toán COD, phí vận chuyển (cash on delivery, shipping fee)
  • Hoàn tiền đơn hàng (order refund)
  • Nhận quà tri ân khách hàng (receive a customer appreciation gift)
  • Cập nhật địa chỉ giao hàng, xác nhận thông tin nhận hàng (update your delivery address, confirm your delivery details)

Some scripts ask you to transfer a small fee to receive the package; others send a fake Shopee link to steal your account, banking details or OTP. Worse, the scammers may know your exact name, phone number and address — collected from the internet or data leaks (see habits that leak your personal data).

Another variation is the “wrong order delivery” (“giao nhầm đơn hàng”): you receive a low-value package but have to pay a fairly large amount for it, and only after opening it do you find a cheap product or something you never ordered.

Safety rule: if you don't remember placing the order, check directly in the Shopee app or the relevant marketplace. Never share your OTP, never log in via a link in a text message, and never transfer money to someone claiming to be a delivery driver to “verify the order”. Delivery companies only deliver packages and collect payment (if any); they never ask you to install an app or read out an OTP.

How to respond when you hear these phrases

When you're on the phone

  1. Don't answer questions about your account number, citizen ID number (CCCD) or OTP.
  2. Hang up — a genuine caller won't force you to “sort it out during this call”.
  3. Call the official hotline yourself (the number on the website, your bill or the back of your bank card) — don't call back the number that just called you.
  4. Tell your family. Scammers insist on secrecy precisely because a second person would see through them immediately.

When you get a text message with a link

  • Don't tap the link; if needed, open the official app yourself or type the website address.
  • Don't install apps from links sent via SMS, Zalo or Messenger.
  • If you've already opened it, check the link with SafePhone or follow our guide on how to check if a website is a scam.

Block scams at the source so you don't have to spot them yourself

Recognizing scams yourself matters, but nobody is alert 100% of the time — especially elderly parents and children. SafePhone reduces how often you have to face scammers at all:

  • Block unknown and scam numbers automatically before your phone rings, based on a community-reported blacklist.
  • Block scam websites: links are checked against a blacklist before they open.
  • Protect your family from scam calls with alerts sent to a shared family channel.

What to do if you've sent money or shared information

  1. Call your bank's official hotline immediately to request a freeze on the receiving account and to lock your cards and online banking. The sooner the better.
  2. Change your passwords for banking, email and social media, and turn on two-factor authentication.
  3. If you installed an unknown app: disconnect from the internet, uninstall the app, and have someone knowledgeable check your phone.
  4. Save the evidence: screenshots of messages, phone numbers, the receiving account number and transfer receipts.
  5. Report it to the nearest police station. Be wary of anyone who contacts you afterward promising to “get your money back” — that's the fund recovery scam.
  6. Report the scam phone numbers and spam or scam text messages to 5656, the hotline run by Vietnam's telecom regulator, and report scam numbers to warn the community.

Conclusion

Phrases like “thu hồi vốn treo” (fund recovery), “lấy lại tiền bị lừa” (getting back money lost to scammers), “làm nhiệm vụ nhận hoa hồng” (do tasks, earn commission), “sở hữu kỳ nghỉ” (timeshare), “lợi nhuận cam kết” (guaranteed returns), “cập nhật sinh trắc học” (biometric update), “tuyển dụng” (recruitment) or “công an điều tra” (police investigation) don't always mean a scam. But when they come with a request to transfer money, share personal information, install an app or tap a strange link, it's almost certainly a scam.

The most important rule: the more urgent, the more secretive and the more unbelievably profitable something sounds, the more likely it is to be a scam — because money is never that easy to make. Share this list with your parents and loved ones.

FAQ

What are the most common scam tactics in 2026?

Common scenarios include: commission-for-tasks scams, easy jobs with high pay and fake recruiters, investments with guaranteed returns, fund recovery offers aimed at previous scam victims, biometric updates via a link, impersonating the police or the procuracy, impersonating the electricity company, and fake Shopee orders or delivery drivers.

Someone called promising to get back the money I lost to a scam. Should I trust them?

No. This is the fund recovery scam aimed at people who have already been scammed. It usually demands application and verification fees and makes victims lose money a second time. If you have been scammed, report it directly to the police.

Do the police call and ask you to transfer money for an investigation?

No. The authorities never ask citizens to transfer money to a personal account for an investigation. If a caller asks you to transfer money and keep it secret, hang up, tell your family and go directly to the nearest police station.

Is a text message asking me to update my biometrics via a link a scam?

Very likely. Only update your biometrics in the official banking app downloaded from the app store or at a bank branch. Don't tap links or install apps sent by text message.

Can I trust a job offer that asks for a training fee or a deposit?

That is a serious red flag. Reputable companies generally don't charge job applicants. Check the job listing on the company's official website and call the company's publicly listed phone number before doing anything.
